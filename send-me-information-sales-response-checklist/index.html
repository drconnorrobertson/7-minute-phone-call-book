7 Responses to “Send Me Information” in Sales

When a prospect says send me information, they may want a useful resource, need time or prefer to end the conversation. Do not assume the request is either a buying signal or a disguised objection. One brief clarifying question can help you respond appropriately without turning a simple request into an interrogation.

Work through the decision

  1. Ask which decision the information supports

    Try: 'What would you like the information to help you decide?' Use the answer to narrow what you send. If the person simply wants a general overview, provide one rather than insisting that they disclose a buying process before receiving a basic explanation.

  2. Offer a choice between two relevant resources

    Try: 'Would a service overview or an example of the implementation steps be more useful?' Only offer resources that actually exist and accurately describe your work. Avoid manufacturing a detailed case study or implying that an illustrative scenario documents a client outcome.

  3. Clarify the requested level of detail

    Try: 'Would you prefer a brief summary or the full scope document?' A busy contact may need a concise explanation they can forward. Match the format to the request rather than sending every available attachment in the hope that one of them creates interest.

  4. Ask whether another reader needs context

    Try: 'Is this for your own review, or should I explain it for someone else as well?' The answer can help you define terms and make the summary self-contained. Do not treat a forwarding request as confirmation that an executive has approved or even seen the project.

  5. Check whether a specific concern is missing

    Try: 'Is there a particular question the information should address?' If the concern is pricing or implementation, answer directly using approved facts. If you do not know, say what you will confirm and when instead of filling the gap with an optimistic estimate.

  6. Ask before scheduling another contact

    Try: 'Would a follow-up be useful after you review it, or would you rather come back to me?' If a follow-up is welcome, agree on its purpose and timing. A resource request alone does not establish an appointment or a commitment to continue the sales process.

  7. Make a clean exit available

    Try: 'If this is not a priority, I can leave the overview with you and close the loop for now.' Use that option sincerely. Record a pause or a no when that is the answer instead of relabeling it as an objection that requires a more persistent sequence.

Worked example

Illustrative exchange: a prospect asks for information because a colleague owns implementation. You ask whether a short implementation summary would help, send the requested document and offer to answer the colleague's questions. The prospect declines a scheduled call. Record the resource request as completed without adding an unconfirmed meeting to the forecast.

Your next step

Choose one clarifying question that fits the moment. Send the promised resource with a short explanation of why it answers the request. Record what was requested, what you sent and whether any further contact was actually agreed. Keep private notes out of forwarded material.

Common questions

Should I use all seven questions on one call?

No. These are alternatives. Ask only what is needed to understand and fulfill the request without prolonging an unwanted conversation.

What if the prospect will not clarify?

Send an appropriate brief overview if you can, respect their preference and avoid inventing a next step they did not agree to.
