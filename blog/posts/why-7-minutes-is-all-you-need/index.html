Why 7 Minutes Is All You Need to Close a Deal

The science behind short, structured calls.

By Dr. Connor Robertson · May 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Most people think closing a deal requires a long, drawn-out conversation. They block an hour for a discovery call, 45 minutes for a follow-up, and another 30 minutes for the close. By the time the deal crosses the finish line, they have invested three or four hours of phone time -- and the prospect has lost half their enthusiasm.

There is a better way. And it takes seven minutes.

The Attention Curve

Human attention follows a predictable curve during any conversation. Engagement peaks in the first two to three minutes, holds steady through minute five or six, and then drops sharply. By minute eight or nine, your prospect's mind has already drifted to their next meeting, their inbox, or what they are having for lunch.

Seven minutes sits right at the edge of peak engagement. It is long enough to build rapport, surface a real problem, present a solution, and lock in a next step. But it is short enough that attention never drops off.

Constraint Creates Clarity

When you know you only have seven minutes, everything changes. You stop rambling about your company history. You skip the small talk that goes nowhere. You ask better questions because you do not have time for bad ones.

The time constraint forces you to be direct, relevant, and valuable. And your prospect notices. They appreciate that you respect their time. That alone sets you apart from 90% of the people calling them.

Shorter Calls, More Calls

Here is the math that most sales professionals ignore: if your average call takes 30 minutes, you can make 10 calls in a five-hour calling block. If your average call takes 7 minutes, you can make 30 or more calls in the same window. Even if your conversion rate is slightly lower per call (which the data does not support), the volume more than makes up for it.

But the real insight is that short, structured calls actually convert at the same rate or higher than long, unstructured ones. You are not sacrificing quality for quantity. You are getting both.

The Psychology of Urgency

When a prospect knows the call is going to be short, they lean in. They pay more attention. They make faster decisions. There is a subtle psychological pressure created by a time-limited conversation that actually helps both parties get to the point.

Compare that to the open-ended call where nobody knows when it will end. Those conversations meander, lose energy, and often end with a vague "let's circle back next week" that never materializes.

How to Use This

Start by telling your prospect how long the call will take. Say something like: "I have a quick thought to run by you -- it will take about seven minutes." This does three things. It earns the yes to the conversation. It sets expectations. And it creates a framework that keeps you both on track.

Then follow the four-step structure: Hook them in the first 30 seconds. Surface their problem. Bridge to your solution. Close with a specific next step. Seven minutes. Done.

The best closers are not the ones who talk the longest. They are the ones who say the right things in the least amount of time.
